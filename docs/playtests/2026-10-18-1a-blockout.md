## What the frames establish

**Close view, a person selected, an order in flight.** The blue ground ring sits at the selected person's feet, the `move` label is clear above the head — not on the ring and not on the path start — and the ribbon runs as a solid yellow band from the ring to a destination knob. The sim's own numbers sit behind it: the snapshot position against the body's global position was **delta 0.0000 m for all three people on every frame**, with the render driver's earlier workaround deleted.

That comparison matters, because before the fix the body and its ring stayed at (0, 0, 0) while the path walked away: the view aimed the body with `look_at` after writing its position, and `look_at` read a stale global transform and wrote the origin back. The harness could never have caught it — the harness asserts the sim, and this was drawing.

**Holding view (orthographic size 42), which is the zoom the acceptance line is about.** No label over the acre (the label hides itself beyond size 30; the HUD names the selected person's job), the ring is fully visible, the ribbon measures about 14 px wide at 1280x720, and the destination is a yellow ball at the ribbon end rather than a few-pixel post.

**A refused order.** The HUD's second line is the sim's own string — `no path` — verbatim, with nothing added, and the selected person carries a red ring.

## What still needs a person

- **Camera feel**: pan speed, the 90-degree Q/E snap, the zoom range, and whether the wide view is the right opening shot. A lavapipe frame at 1.3 s cannot answer any of that.
- **Whether the destination knob is obvious without a caption.** It reads as a ball on the end of the band. It may want to be a stake.
- **Whether the close-zoom label sitting on a neighbour standing behind is acceptable** — it is a no-depth-test billboard, so it can overlap whoever is behind the selected person.
- **A colonist at holding zoom is a handful of pixels.** The ring is what makes selection readable, not the body. Worth confirming that this is the intended read.
- Real resolution and real frame pacing, on the machine that will actually play it.

## A defect the first playtest found

**What was reported.** A person played the blockout and reported that right-clicking the ground did not move them.

**What a reproduction showed.** The first order, at tick 6, was accepted and the person walked. The same order at tick 48 failed as `hungry`. Hunger had fallen below 0.35 after 39 ticks, under four seconds of play, and every move and gather after that was refused. The acre yields no food, and the cast was carrying nothing, so hunger did not recover.

**The cause.** The root cause was a contradictory sentence in the contract. It said hunger falls by 1/60 per tick, so a full person is hungry in about 1.7 days at 1x. 1/60 per tick empties a person in 60 ticks, six seconds at 10 Hz. The sim implemented the per-tick rate.

**The correction.** The rate is now measured in days. Hunger falls by 1/14400 per tick, and a full person is empty after two days. The first hunger refusal after the fix lands within a tick or two of tick 9360, about sixteen minutes of play. The cast now starts with four grain each, so an eat order can succeed.

**Verified by the same reproduction.** The order that failed at tick 48 now walks the person to its target with a path and no failure text, and the gate is green: the sim tests, the smoke line, and every bridge check through the headless harness.
