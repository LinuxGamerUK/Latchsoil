# Lore

## What happened

The Gramarye could not feed itself. It hung in orbit over Holl and bought grain from a heavy moon catalogued Latch 11, the latch that held the ring's hunger shut. Companies, churches, and penal boards dropped charters onto it: a sealed people, a job, a seed bank, a law, and a return schedule.

The ring paid in tools, medicine, and edits. Edited workers were cheaper than new ships. Over two hundred years the edits bred true. The law still called everyone charter-folk. Bodies disagreed.

Then the ring's inner rail failed. For eleven days glass, keel-metal, frozen orchards, and whole neighborhoods fell. People named it the Shedding. Dust changed the rains. Some charters died in the first Deep Bell. Some ate their own laws. Some became the folk below.

The sky is still wrong. At dusk a pale arc of debris, the Stitch, hangs where the ring used to be whole. Old machines still drink glass chits cut from that debris. Nobody on the Latch makes a new microchip. They recut old ones. Bows, black powder, coil-tools, and half-dead servitors share the same shed.

There is no magic. What outsiders call miracles are edits, fungus, sound, and machines whose manuals burned.

## What the player is granted

Someone who still pretends to keep the old books grants a dead survey plot. The run starts in Sprout with a peg in the dirt, a cart, and a handful of one people. The default people are the Harth.

The Gramarye does not get repaired. The Stitch stays weather. Thriving means a holding that can feed itself, defend its ditch, and choose which promises it keeps.

## The two tracks

Good and evil are records of specific acts.

**Kept word** grows when the holding honors guest-right, shelters refugees, trades fairly, shares water, or keeps a defense pact after it costs grain.

**Open appetite** grows when the holding raids, takes forced labor, strips implants from the dead or the captured, uses plague or fire on fields, takes flesh as ration, or builds a crown out of fear.

A holding can carry both records. A merciful people with a closed ditch, and a generous people who will butcher a prisoner, are different neighbors. Factions remember the act, not a blended karma score.

The picture of a cruel act stays at the record, the law, and the consequence: a missing person, a new ration in the store, a field burned, a treaty broken, a neighbor's relation number, a line of dialogue. The game does not stage graphic punishment or body horror.

## Endings

These exist as stubs in milestone 7, after the political systems can produce them.

- **Open House.** You fed strangers all year. You are loved, and you are used.
- **Quiet Ditch.** You refused both sainthood and conquest. You are still standing, and alone.
- **Red Granary.** Your people are fed because other people were the ration.
- **Stitch Throne.** The holdings pay tribute, and your weapons are cut from the sky.
- **Second Charter.** Three other peoples sign a law you published, and mean it.
- **Ash Orchard.** You burned the fertile belt so an enemy could not have it. You won a dead map.

## How the year presses

Hunger should bite on day 2 if nobody finds food. Forage bridges a careful holding to the first harvest. A planted plot pays about five days after planting, so a season can carry two crops. Four people need about four worked plots and a well. Four people eat eighty grain in a Sprout and four plots can pay twice that, so the margin belongs to seed, spoilage and the storehouse. The headless sim locks those sentences with a test in milestone 3, and the test is allowed to adjust the exact yield until the pressure feels like that paragraph.

Glass chits are the only coin, and smelters eat them.

The survey peg stays. Old ring law says so. The ditch around the peg is the lived border. Pushing the ditch past the granted acre without a new paper is a political act. That is how a farmer's game becomes a war game.

## Incidents the world can throw

Drawn from the seed and the neighbors, once the event deck exists:

- Shedding aftershock
- A refugee line
- Blight or spore bloom
- Shardbeast migration
- A servitor still following a dead work order
- Choir night market
- Drought
- A guest who is hunted
- A printer-loom that wakes hungry
- A ditch-mark accusation

## Voice

People on the Latch talk like neighbors with jobs, not like prophecy. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. The sample lines in the race doc are the accent. Match them when writing new lines.

A Harth says: "The peg stays. The ditch moves when the paper says."

A Cindervault says: "Cold food is a confession."
